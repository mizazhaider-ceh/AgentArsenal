Cost Benefit Framework

Use this when deciding whether an engagement, a tool build, or a process change is worth doing. It forces the costs and the benefits into the same units so the comparison is honest.

Step 1: Name the decision

One line. "Run a two-week external pentest of client acme." "Build an automated report generator." If it does not fit one line, it is two decisions.

Step 2: List the costs (be pessimistic)

| Cost | Estimate | Notes | | --- | --- | --- | | Operator time | ___ hours x ___ rate | Include prep, execution, reporting, rework. | | Tooling and infra | $___ | Licenses, cloud hosts, lab time. | | Client disruption | ___ hours of their staff | Interviews, firewall changes, incident triage if we trip alarms. | | Risk reserve | score from RISK_MATRIX.md | Convert High/Extreme bands into contingency time or insurance. | | Opportunity cost | ___ | What we do not do while doing this. |

Rule: double the time estimate for any task you have not done twice before.

Step 3: List the benefits (be skeptical)

| Benefit | Estimate | Notes | | --- | --- | --- | | Findings prevented | $___ | Expected loss avoided. Use the client's incident history, not industry averages, when you have it. | | Compliance value | pass/fail | Some work is worth doing because a contract or regulation demands it. Say so plainly. | | Capability gained | ___ | Skills, tools, or playbooks the team keeps afterward. Real, reusable, named. | | Relationship value | ___ | Trust earned with this client. Do not put a dollar figure on it; describe it. |

Rule: a benefit you cannot name specifically is not a benefit, it is a hope. Hopes go in the risks column as uncertainty.

Step 4: Compare

  • If total benefit clearly exceeds total cost: Go, and say which benefit

    • carries the decision.

  • If they are close: Go only if the top risk has a mitigation you trust, or

    • shrink the scope until the math is clear.

  • If cost exceeds benefit: No-Go, or redesign. "We already started" is not

    • a benefit.

Step 5: Record

One paragraph in the engagement or project log: the decision, the two or three numbers that carried it, and the date. Revisit when a number changes by more than half; a framework you never recheck is decoration.

Worked mini-example

Decision: build an automated PDF report generator (one week of work).

  • Costs: 40 hours x internal rate, plus 10 hours of rework reserve (never

    • built one twice before, so time is doubled already). Total: 50 hours.

  • Benefits: each manual report takes 6 hours; the team writes ~20 reports a

    • year. 120 hours saved yearly, minus 10 hours yearly maintenance = 110 hours. Capability gained: a reusable tool for every future engagement.

  • Compare: 50 hours once vs 110 hours every year. Go. The carrying benefit

    • is the yearly time saving; the capability gain is the tiebreaker if the numbers were close.
